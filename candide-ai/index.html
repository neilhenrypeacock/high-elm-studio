I grew up on a small family farm in rural England, and have spent the last fourteen years working in luxury travel. At The Newt, and again at Babylonstoren, I felt both of those worlds in one place: the working land I come from, and the art of looking after a guest. When I saw what your role is setting out to achieve, I knew I wanted to share my ideas with you. This is that. I hope you find it interesting.

Neil Peacock
The estate

People come to Babylonstoren for…

The garden, the wine, Babel, Soetmelksvlei and the Greenhouse. A magical atmosphere, a farm-to-fork ethos where nothing travels far, and the feeling of being looked after at every step.

Every part of a visit is well timed, considered in every detail and personal. The online experience should mirror that: the same timing, the same attention to detail, the same personal touch, from the first post someone sees to years after they leave.

The question How does the online experience become a mirror of the offline one?

The journey · Part 01 of 09 · Two guests

Two guests, one production process

My assumption: two guests matter most.

The international, peak-season guest, who could visit every estate once and become a lifelong guest. And the seasonal, mostly domestic guest, who books shoulder season and comes back every year. Less per booking, far more over a lifetime. One process, two sequences. Everything that follows is designed around these two guests.

What it builds Messages shaped by how each guest behaves, not just their name at the top of an email.
The journey · Part 02 of 09 · Discover

Six doorways into the same world

The six places someone is most likely to discover Babylonstoren for the first time.

Social, paid, press, search, YouTube and word of mouth can and should work in tandem, with each other and with the seasons. Each would carry the same voice, the same season, the same sense of place.

What it builds A first impression that feels like being there, so the experience starts before anyone books.

Shown in the diagram:

  • The six doorways lead to one prospect.
The journey · Part 03 of 09 · Booking

Two landing pages, each designed for action

Every doorway leads to one of two pages.

One is designed to enquire and book. The other is designed to sign up to the welcome series, a journey that keeps going and responds to what each person does. Each gets its own version of retargeting, for those who looked but did not act. I write, design, code and connect all of it: the pages, the emails, the automations and the data behind them.

What it builds Two clear next steps, and a follow-up for everyone who pauses.
The journey · Part 03 · Before and during the stay

Once they’ve booked, keep it light

A few delicate, well-timed messages around the stay.

One month out, what will be in season when they arrive. Ten days out, help planning the days: tours, tables, the spa. The day before, a short guide to their stay. During the stay, the estate does the talking. Afterwards, a thank you, and a week later, a short, personal request for feedback.

The journey · Part 04 of 09 · After the stay

The stay is the start, not the end

Two paths from checkout, timed to each guest’s next booking window.

Come back to Babylonstoren next season and see a different side of the same place. Or discover a sister estate in the season you love: summer at The Newt, autumn at Vignamaggio, winter at Over-Amstel Boerderij, spring at Blou. Retargeting follows each guest’s booking cycle, so the invitation arrives just as they are ready to plan again.

What it builds Repeat visits, referrals, and guests who know every estate.

The fuel · Part 05 of 09 · The season

This month at Babylonstoren

A framework like this only works with consistent creative input. It is led by a monthly long-form video series: behind the scenes meets a cinematic depiction of life at Babylonstoren.

Each month, the estates are seen through the view of that month: what is in season on the land, what is going into the restaurants, what guests can do right now. One steady stream does two jobs at once: evergreen content that meets each guest at the right moment in their own journey, and broadcast on social that keeps growing audience and reach. It is rich, immersive farm-to-fork content that changes with the seasons, the produce and the experiences: the garden walk, the chef’s morning, the water buffalo, the light. The same places and people return each month, so the story deepens over seasons and years.

Building on your work Babylonstoren’s YouTube already holds great stories. The format is what changes: one month at a time, so there is always a reason to come back. Clarkson’s Farm and Thomas Straker’s (Not) Straker’s Farm show how much people love following a farm through a year.

The twelve months of the series:

  • January — Summer fruit ripens in the orchards
  • February — The grape harvest begins
  • March — Harvest comes into the cellar
  • April — Olives are picked and pressed
  • May — Autumn colour across the vines
  • June — Winter citrus in the garden
  • July — Pruning the vines by hand
  • August — Soup and bread from the bakery
  • September — Blossom across the orchards
  • October — Planting the spring beds
  • November — Farm-to-fork in November
  • December — Long summer lunches outside
The fuel · Part 05 · The people

The people, and the stories that return

Returning characters and storylines make it feel like a true place, one audiences can connect with and follow.

Constance, the gardener. The chefs in the kitchen. The winemaker through a year in the cellar. The farm team and the animals. The hotel staff who look after every guest. Each month picks up where the last left off, so viewers return to see what happened next, and the estate becomes somewhere they know before they arrive.

What it builds An audience that feels it knows the place, and the people, before it books.

The fuel · Part 06 of 09 · The feast

A feast to finish every season

Four feasts a year, one to close each season, at every estate.

We film every month, so by the end of a season there is a campaign ready to cut. Each one closes at a feast: the harvest table in summer, olives and new wine in autumn, around the fire in winter, the first of the garden in spring. Every dish on the table has been followed through the season. The Horrell & Horrell collaboration dinner is close to what I mean.

The engine · Part 07 of 09 · How I build it

From data to revenue

Data first, then the infrastructure, then creative messages tested until they deliver revenue.

Guest data shows where people hesitate, what they book together and when they come back. That shapes the infrastructure: segments, triggers and timing. Creative messages are then A/B tested within it, and the version that converts becomes the standard, so every journey keeps improving. With AI, the infrastructure can now be built in a fraction of the time, so the value moves to what you say, and how well you test it.

The engine · Part 08 of 09 · What it produces

One creative video brief, a year of series

Filming one long-form film each month creates far more than the film itself: it opens up a whole range of other content, in every format. The series alongside are examples.

The monthly long-form series is the source. From each shoot comes a variety of other content: a weekly Instagram reel and YouTube Shorts, life on the farm, the restaurant from garden to plate, a year of wine, a year of olive oil, farm-to-fork in November. Every piece is cut again into long form, shorts, SEO articles, email sequences and seasonal paid media. And because every estate shoots to the same brief, it can be packaged across them too: September at Babylonstoren, The Newt and Vignamaggio would make a beautiful film on its own.

What it builds Series like a year of wine sell both the hotel and the shop.

The engine · Part 09 of 09 · Measure

Every part has a hypothesis

What we expect, what happened, what changes next.

A brief first. Goals, not tasks. The full lifecycle. What works at one estate, shared across all. True to each brand. A hypothesis for every campaign, A/B tests, and learnings that compound.

The loop

One cycle that strengthens every season

Creative messaging fuels the journey. Personal, automated content supports it. The guest books. Post-trip content brings them back, and their referrals with them.

All nine parts run as one system, and each turn of the loop makes the next one stronger. With what AI now makes possible, the focus can stay on a few key frameworks done well, then scale them very effectively, from one estate to every one.

Proof · A safari operator

I’ve just built this

Using AI, I recently built a complete customer journey selling luxury honeymoons across South Africa. I wrote, designed, coded and connected all of it myself, with no additional design, editing or development support. It has been live for six weeks and is returning 75× its ad spend.

Not long ago this would have needed developers and designers. The full journey: paid creative, landing page, welcome series, a kick-back email for those who enquired, and enquiry data fed straight into the system. Those who viewed but did not book were entered into an elegant retargeting campaign.
